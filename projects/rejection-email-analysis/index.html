After Clicking Apply

I can get a reply on the day I apply or wait more than three months. I track 342 applications in Luxembourg to see how often I hear back and what employers say when they turn me down.

About the data

I apply mostly through employers’ careers pages. My tracker starts in November 2025, and I last checked the tracker and email archive on November 24, 2026. I use original emails for send times and wording. Dated copies and employer records fill in some earlier decisions.

Applications and rejections by month

I record 176 rejections. July has the most applications, with 81. October has the most dated rejections, with 42.

How far I get

For 116 applications (33.9%), I have a dated reply. I record a next step for 20 applications (5.8%), from a recruiter’s question to a test or interview.

I can verify interview invitations for nine applications, including interviews still to come.

How long I wait

The median wait for a first reply is 13.5 days. Seven replies arrive on the day I apply, and 13 take at least 90 days.

For rejections with a documented date, the median wait is 17 days. Five arrive the day I apply, and 21 take at least 90 days.

When there is no reply

Of the 179 applications at least 90 days old, 68 (38%) are still open with no substantive reply in my records.

The longest wait I can measure ends with a rejection 193 days after I apply.

When emails arrive

I have 100 rejection emails to compare. All but one arrive between August 7 and November 24, 2026. I take the time of the April email from a copy of the original.

Tuesday has the most emails, with 25. The busiest hour is 10 to 11 a.m., with 23.

What the emails say

I find a thank-you in 62 of the 63 English emails. Across all 100 emails, 45 give no reason and 32 say the employer is moving ahead with other candidates. Seven mention specific experience or qualifications.

I compare each email with its closest match in the same language. Forty-nine have a close match. For 17 of those, I can verify that at least one close match comes from a different employer.

Other applicants have tracked the same kind of email. Liz Dugan also finds that most of hers give no reason or mention other candidates, using her own categories. Dugan’s rejection analysis

Roles and employer size

Data and technical roles make up the largest group in my November 17 comparison. I record 62 rejections from 125 applications in that group and 45 from 81 in finance and investment.

I find published staff counts for nine employers in the November 17 snapshot, covering 39 applications. Where available, I use the headcount for the company or group.
